# Sentiment-Based Trading Guide

*News • Market Sentiment • Options • Price Action • Entry Timing*

**Purpose:** A practical framework for trading US and Canadian equities using market sentiment as the starting signal, with price, volume, technical confirmation, and risk management used to validate entries. This guide consolidates the discussion and is intended as an educational framework—not personalized financial advice.

## 1. Core Principle

Do not trade simply because a headline is bullish or bearish. The objective is to identify changes in market expectations before or as price fully reflects them.

A useful mental model:

> Information → Expectations → Market reaction → Price/volume confirmation → Entry → Risk management

## 3. What to Track

- Macro: S&P 500/Nasdaq, VIX, US 10-year yield, USD, oil, gold, Fed expectations, CPI/PCE, employment data.
- Sector: Determine whether the whole sector supports or contradicts the individual stock.
- Company: earnings, guidance, analyst revisions, insider transactions, institutional activity, options, and news.
- Crowd: Reddit, Stocktwits, X/social discussion, and search interest. Treat crowd sentiment as a secondary signal.

## 4. Sentiment Is About Expectations vs. Reality

A company can report excellent results and still fall if the market expected even better results. The important question is not simply “Is the news good?” but “Is the news better or worse than what was already priced in?”

Example: EPS expectation = $1.00; actual EPS = $1.20. If the stock falls, investigate guidance, valuation, margins, or other expectations that disappointed.

## 5. Options Sentiment

- Monitor unusual call/put activity, implied volatility, open interest, large blocks, and options skew.
- Never assume large call buying is automatically bullish; trades can be hedges, spreads, or part of larger strategies.
- Use options flow as confirmation rather than a standalone buy signal.

## 6. Insider and Analyst Signals

- SEC EDGAR is a primary source for company filings and insider transactions.
- One insider sale does not automatically mean bearish sentiment.
- Multiple insiders buying after a significant decline can be more informative.
- Track analyst upgrades, downgrades, target changes, and—most importantly—changes in consensus expectations.

## 7. Best Entry Timing During the US Trading Day

For Calgary, the US market currently opens at 7:30 AM Mountain Time and closes at 2:00 PM Mountain Time during the relevant daylight-saving period. Always verify the current offset when daylight-saving changes.

| Time (ET) | Typical behavior | Guideline | Rating |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 9:30–9:45 | Very high volatility; overnight orders and gaps | Usually observe; avoid impulsive entries | ⚠️ |
| 9:45–10:30 | Initial direction becomes clearer | Preferred confirmation window | ★★★★★ |
| 10:30–11:30 | Trend may continue; volatility often falls | Good if setup remains valid | ★★★★☆ |
| 11:30–1:30 | Often lower volume | Usually avoid new trades unless catalyst is strong | ★★☆☆☆ |
| 1:30–3:00 | Activity can increase | Good secondary window | ★★★★☆ |
| 3:00–4:00 | Closing positioning and volume | Can work, but manage volatility | ★★★★☆ |

## 8. Opening-Range + Sentiment Confirmation Setup

- Before the open: identify stocks with meaningful catalysts and strong sentiment.
- At the open: do not automatically buy the gap. Let the first 15 minutes establish an opening range.
- After the first 15 minutes: look for price holding or reclaiming VWAP, strong volume, sector confirmation, and a break of the opening range.
- A stronger setup is: new positive catalyst + bullish sentiment + sector confirmation + price/volume confirmation.
- If good news is followed by persistent selling, respect the market reaction. The market may be saying the news was already priced in or was not good enough.

## 9. Friday vs. Other Days

There is no reliable rule that Friday is always a bad day to enter or Monday is always a good day. Trade the catalyst and setup, not the weekday.

- Friday morning can provide valid setups.
- Late-Friday new swing positions carry additional weekend gap/event risk.
- Be more cautious when the thesis depends on a weekend-sensitive event such as geopolitical developments, major announcements, or binary decisions.
- A strong Tuesday catalyst with confirmation is preferable to a weak Monday setup simply because Monday is the first trading day.

## 11. Practical Sentiment Score

A starting framework for research/backtesting—not a proven predictive model:

| Signal | Example weight | What to assess |
| --- | --- | --- |
| News/catalyst | +2 | Is the information genuinely new and above/below expectations? |
| Analyst revisions | +2 | Are estimates/targets moving in the same direction? |
| Earnings surprise/guidance | +2 | Did actual results and guidance beat the expectations bar? |
| Options flow | +1 | Is positioning consistent with the thesis? |
| Insider activity | +1 | Are meaningful insider transactions supportive? |
| Social sentiment | +1 | Is attention/sentiment unusually strong? |
| Price/volume confirmation | +2 | Does price action validate the thesis? |

Illustrative interpretation: 8–11 = strong setup to investigate; 5–7 = watch; 3–4 = neutral/weak; 0–2 = avoid. These thresholds and weights must be tested with historical data before being used with real money.

## 12. Signal Hierarchy

For a sentiment-driven strategy, prioritize:

1. Unexpected catalyst
2. Market/sector reaction
3. Price + volume confirmation
4. Options positioning
5. Analyst revisions
6. Social sentiment

## 13. Entry Checklist

- I can identify the catalyst.
- The catalyst is genuinely new or materially different from expectations.
- Overall market sentiment supports the trade—or I have a clear reason it does not need to.
- The sector confirms the stock thesis.
- Price action confirms the sentiment.
- Volume is meaningful.
- Price is behaving constructively around VWAP/opening range/key levels.
- I know the invalidation/stop level before entering.
- The expected reward is at least roughly 2× the planned risk.
- I am not entering solely because of social-media hype.
- The position size is appropriate for the risk.
